Fix the occasion and budget first

Ask three questions: is this for everyday drinking, a family meal, a gathering with friends, or a gift? Do the people at the table usually drink baijiu? How much do you expect to drink? The occasion sets aroma type and price band, the amount sets volume, and gifting sets packaging requirements.

The budget does not need to be settled in one go. For a first purchase, split it into "one bottle to try" and "compare later". Buying one mainstream bottle and deciding afterwards which aroma type you prefer is more reliable than buying a whole case. Prices move with channel and vintage, so no figure is given here.

Learn the aroma types before the brands

Baijiu is classified by aroma type, with strong aroma, light aroma, sauce aroma and rice aroma among the common ones. Aroma type describes a process and flavour direction, not a quality grade. For a first drink, do not chase "the most authentic version of one type"; find the aroma intensity and sweetness you can accept.

Aroma direction Typical flavours Common strength Note for beginners
Strong aroma Pit aroma, grain aroma, mellow sweetness 38%–52% ABV Expressive and easy to recognise
Light aroma Clean, fresh, grain aroma 40%–53% ABV Direct entry, noticeable heat
Sauce aroma Sauce, roasted, full-bodied 53% ABV common Complex; may feel heavy at first
Rice aroma Rice aroma, soft sweetness Often lower strength Gentle, lighter aroma

For strong aroma, start with the overview of strong-aroma baijiu, then pick a specific bottle. You do not need all four types at once; two for comparison is enough.

Strength, volume and label

Alcohol by volume is commonly 38%–53%. Higher strength means more heat and faster aroma release; lower strength enters more softly but may have thinner aroma layers. For a first purchase, choose either 38%–42% ABV or a mainstream 52% ABV, and do not buy several strengths at once.

The common volume is 500 mL, with small bottles and gift packs also available. A small bottle suits tasting, while 500 mL suits a family meal. The label should be checked for producer, product name, aroma type, strength, net volume, product standard, production date or batch and anti-counterfeit information. Similar packaging does not mean the same product; channel and batch are more reliable.

A three-step buying checklist

  1. Set the occasion: a mainstream bottle for everyday drinking, gift packaging only for gifting.
  2. Set the aroma type: choose one expressive, easy-to-recognise direction first, then compare with another.
  3. Set the strength: choose either 38%–42% ABV or 52% ABV, and do not buy too much at once.
  4. Check the label: producer, aroma type, strength, net volume, standard, batch, anti-counterfeit.
  5. Choose the channel: a reputable supermarket, the brand's official shop or an authorised channel, and keep the receipt.

For a single entry bottle, the strong-aroma route includes Wuliangye Puwu, Guojiao 1573, Crystal Jiannanchun and Gujing Gongjiu Gu20; the feng-aroma route includes Hong Xifeng; the light-aroma route includes Qinghua Fen 30. These are specific products, not aroma types themselves.

How to drink without wasting the first bottle

Baijiu is suited to small cups and slow sipping, and ice is not recommended. Ice suppresses aroma and makes the relationship between heat and sweetness harder to judge. Room temperature, small sips and food are a more stable introduction. The glass need not be elaborate: a clean small cup with a slightly narrowed rim is enough, as covered in how to choose a glass.

For the first cup, smell first, then take a small sip and let the liquid rest on the tongue, noting the order of sweetness, heat, bitterness and returning sweetness. Home-style dishes, braised food, nuts and red-braised dishes pair well; heavy spice masks aroma. It is easy to overdrink baijiu, so prepare water and control the pace rather than trying to "train" your tolerance.

Common mistakes

First, looking only at packaging and price instead of the label. Second, treating aroma type as a grade. Third, buying a high-strength bottle first and drinking it fast. Fourth, diluting heavily with ice or soft drinks and hiding the drink itself. Fifth, treating "vintage" or "cellar age" as the age of the liquid. Sixth, drinking quickly on an empty stomach.

Budget and buying

For a first purchase, prefer a mainstream specification and a reputable channel rather than a scarce edition. If the budget is limited, buy a small bottle or a single bottle first and decide on a repeat purchase after tasting. For gifting, consider gift boxes, bags and receipts; for everyday drinking, put the budget into the liquid rather than the packaging.

Store away from light in a cool place, upright, and tighten the cap after opening. Protect bottles from shock and heat when travelling. Keep the label and receipt so that authenticity questions can be checked later.
