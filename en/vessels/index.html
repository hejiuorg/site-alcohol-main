Absinthe Glass

A glass with a wide bowl and a narrow rim, usually paired with a carved sugar spoon. It is used for the absinthe water ritual, where the drink turns from green to milky white.

On this page · 6Read GlasswareReviewedEN

Beer Mug

A large beer vessel with a handle and thick walls, common in German beer culture. Thick walls hold the cold, the handle insulates the hand and the wide mouth invites a big draught.

On this page · 6Read GlasswareReviewedEN

Champagne flute

A tall, narrow, slightly tapered stemmed glass used for Champagne and sparkling wine; the narrow body helps retain bubbles but opens aroma less than wider shapes.

On this page · 8Read GlasswareReviewedEN

Copita

A small stemmed tulip-shaped glass used for tasting Sherry in Spain, with a gathered bowl and a slightly flared rim, typically holding about 100–150 ml; also used for nosing some spirits.

Coupe glass

A stemmed, shallow bowl-shaped glass used for Champagne, sparkling wine and short drinks served without ice; the wide rim opens the aroma but lets bubbles fade quickly.

Decanter

A glass vessel for separating sediment and letting wine meet air, usually with a wide base, a long neck and a stopper. Decanting time depends on age, body and aroma.

Goblet

A stemmed glass with a wide bowl and an open rim, used for Belgian abbey and Trappist ales, some wines and table water; capacities commonly run from 250 to 500 ml.

Hurricane Glass

A tall glass with a slender body, a bulging middle and a flared rim, shaped like an old hurricane lamp. It is used for tropical cocktails and crushed-ice drinks.

Julep Cup

A small metal cup, usually silver or pewter, used for the mint julep. Metal conducts cold quickly, frosts the wall and keeps the drink icy with crushed ice and mint.

Martini glass

A conical, stemmed short-drink glass with a slightly narrowed rim, also called a cocktail glass, used for the Dry Martini, Margarita and Daiquiri; capacity has no single standard and pre-chilling matters more than shape.

Nick and Nora glass

Named after the detective couple in the 1934 film The Thin Man; a small stemmed glass with a shallow bowl and wide rim, typically holding about 150–200 ml and suited to short drinks served without ice.

Pint glass

A straight-sided beer glass holding 568 ml in the UK and 473 ml in the US; conical, nonic and handled jug versions are common, and capacity varies by market and product.

Red Wine Glass

A stemmed glass with a wide bowl and a slightly narrowed rim, used for red wine. The wide bowl opens the aroma and the narrowed rim gathers it at the nose.

Shot Glass

A small, thick-walled glass used for one-swallow servings of spirits, for measuring and for layered cocktails. It is built for convenience and intensity, not aroma.

Snifter

A short-stemmed glass with a large bowl and a narrowed rim, traditionally used for Cognac and brandy; typical capacity is 300–800 ml, while the actual pour is far smaller.

Tasting glass

The small tulip-shaped glass defined by ISO 3591, commonly called the INAO glass, with a nominal capacity of 215 ml; used for comparative tasting of wine, spirits and sake.

Tiki Mug

A shaped ceramic or glass mug carved as a tiki god, mask or totem. It belongs to tropical cocktail culture, and its capacity and form are more theatrical than an ordinary glass.

Tulip glass

A stemmed glass with a wide bowl that narrows towards the rim, used for Champagne, beer and some short drinks; it balances aroma concentration and bubble retention.

Whisky Nosing Glass

A small tasting glass with a tulip-shaped bowl and a narrowed rim, used for nosing and sipping whisky. The rim gathers aroma and the short base fits the palm.

White Wine Glass

A stemmed glass with a smaller bowl and a narrower rim, used for white wine. The smaller bowl limits oxidation and the narrowed rim holds the floral and acidic freshness.
