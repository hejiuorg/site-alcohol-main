Bottle Opener

A simple tool for crown-cap beer bottles, common as a bar blade, wall-mounted opener or key shape. It grips the cap's lower edge and lifts steadily.

On this page · 6Read ToolsReviewedEN

Channel knife

A bar knife with a small V- or U-shaped notch that pulls long, even strips of citrus peel; notch width, sharpness and cutting depth determine aroma release and whether the garnish holds its shape.

On this page · 9Read ToolsReviewedEN

Citrus press

A manual tool that uses a lever or screw mechanism to press half a citrus fruit; cup size, stability and acid-resistant materials determine juice yield, cleaning and service life.

Cocktail pick

A thin pin used to hold olives, cherries, citrus peel or onions; common lengths run 10–15 cm, and material, length, point treatment and corrosion resistance decide whether it holds the garnish without getting in the way.

Corkscrew

A tool that uses a helix and a lever to pull a cork. The screw must be centred, the pull vertical and slow, and a broken cork needs care.

Fine strainer

The second strainer in double straining, used to catch fruit fibre, herb fragments and ice shards; mesh fineness, frame stability and cleaning affect clarity and speed.

Hydrometer

A hydrometer is a glass instrument that reads liquid density by buoyancy; common scales show density, specific gravity, alcohol strength or sugar content, and it must be used with temperature correction because it measures density, not alcohol or sugar directly.

Ice Bucket

A bucket-shaped vessel for chilling bottles, usually metal or glass. An ice-water mix chills faster than ice alone, the water should cover the wine and the time depends on the drink.

Mixing glass

Muddler

Speed pourer

A spout fitted to a spirit bottle that restricts flow and allows one-handed, continuous pouring; it buys speed and consistency, not accuracy, which still belongs to the jigger.

Strainer

A bar tool that keeps ice, crushed ice and solid residue out of the glass; Hawthorne and julep strainers are the common types, with a fine-mesh strainer used for a second pass.

Thermometer

A tool for measuring liquid, bottle or cellar temperature, common as a probe, infrared or stick-on model. Readings must settle, calibration must be checked and the probe placed where it represents the whole.

A two-handed peeler with a Y- or T-shaped handle that cuts wide, thin ribbons of citrus peel or vegetable skin; blade type, cutting gap and pressure decide thickness and how much pith comes along.
